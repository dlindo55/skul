Tips for Scroll-stopping Insta Lives and Stories

PHOTOGRAPHIC EDUCATIONEDUCATIONBUSINESSMARKETINGSOCIAL MEDIA
Written By michelle mckay

Going live on Instagram is a fabulous way for you to connect meaningfully with the audience of your photography business, but when you’re new to showing up with your face it can feel a little daunting. 

Thing is though, when you actually bother to brush your hair and change out of your Ooodie, you kind of want it to be worth your while! Here are some practical suggestions to ensure your viewers remain engaged and watching when you make the effort to touch base with them live or by video.

Start Strong, Hook Instantly

Scrolling on socials is habitual and mindless, so you’ll need to be intentional about capturing the attention of your viewers within that first 3 precious seconds to ensure they don’t keep moving. 

Begin in a way that is prompt and compelling, with an engaging expression and a strong opening.

Sharing a question or statement that reveals the PURPOSE of your live in that first few seconds can help your viewer know what’s in it for THEM if they stick around.

Show your Enthusiasm 

I *hate* to be that creeper telling you to ‘smile more’, but when you aren’t with your audience IRL, you really do need to exude a positive energy that sets a welcoming tone.

It’s so common to see a photographer going live, and their face is deadpan and solemn (my concentrating face is, too!).

The reality is, from an audience perspective it’s hard to want to choose to watch that, when if you keep scrolling you’ll see bright smiling faces that will give you the dopamine boost your body is craving. 

So! Consciously choose to smile, maintain eye contact, and use hand gestures to convey your confidence and warmth.

Keep It Short and Sweet

Stay Concise and aim for bite sized.

Depending on the content this could be 20 seconds, or it could be 5 minutes.

The most important thing to keep in mind is to make sure whatever you’re saying is high value.

Focus on one main point or theme to keep it easy to digest, and follow a structure (tell them what you’re going to do and then do it eg Here are my 3 top tips for a happy newborn - Tip 1, Tip 2, Tip 3), and get to your point quickly - avoid drawn out introductions and filler. 

Consider the Visual

For enjoyable watching, good lighting is important.

Use natural light by positioning yourself beside a window. This not only looks professional but also aligns with your natural, organic style. Make sure your background is clean and uncluttered, too.

Consider the Sound

Sound is EVERYTHING.

Make sure you can be clearly heard, and that the listening experience will be pleasant and uninhibited.

Speak clearly, and especially avoid speaking too slowly - if you draw out your thoughts too much, you’ll lose the attention of your viewer. Gather your thoughts BEFORE you go live, and write some pointers on a piece of paper if it will help. Maintain a peppy, conversational tone to keep viewers engaged.

Be Human

Being yourself is key to building a meaningful connection with your audience, so keep it relaxed, as if you were chatting with someone you’ve just bumped into at the post office or grocery store.

Invite Engagement

Ask for opinions and encourage interaction to keep the conversation flowing, or show them you’re involved with them in a meaningful way by referencing the comment / real life interaction that motivated you going live in your stories in the first place. 

Close strong

Restate your key points to reinforce your message, or end with a clear call to action that encourages further engagement and interaction.

Practice makes Perfect

The best solution to feeling stiff or wobbly, or like your nerves make you seem too serious or slow, is to make Instagram Stories more often. Set yourself the challenge of going live once a day for a week, and by the end of the 7 days I promise you’ll have noticed a massive change to your confidence and ability.
