1. The Hero’s Journey

Best for: Emotional brand storytelling, customer transformation arcs, longer-form content (videos, blogs, case studies, Instagram reels, TikToks).

What It Is:

Originally a storytelling structure from mythology (think: Star Wars, The Lion King), the Hero’s Journey is a 12-stage narrative framework that follows a character through a challenge, transformation, and return. In digital marketing, we simplify this to 3–5 major phases to show how a product or brand helps a “hero” (your customer) evolve.

Who’s the Hero?

Your customer — not your brand. Your brand is the guide (think: Yoda, not Luke Skywalker). Your content should show how your brand helps the customer conquer a problem and transform.

How to Use It in Digital Marketing:

  • Start with a relatable struggle or goal

  • Introduce your product or service as the guide or catalyst

  • Show how the customer transforms (solves the problem, gains confidence, etc.)

  • End with the emotional payoff or success story

Example:

  • Instagram Reel for a fitness brand:

    1. “I couldn’t walk up the stairs without getting winded.”

    2. “Then I joined the 30-day movement challenge.”

    3. “Now I run 3 miles before breakfast and feel stronger than ever.”
      (Hero’s transformation is the arc; brand is the guide.)

2. Before–After–Bridge

Best for: Social media captions, landing pages, email campaigns, short storytelling arcs.

What It Is:

A straightforward, high-impact content formula that shows your audience a contrast between where they are now and where they could be — with your product/service as the bridge. It's a quick transformation framework that's ideal for visual storytelling and copywriting.

The Structure:

  • Before: Describe the audience’s current state or pain point

  • After: Paint a picture of what life looks like after the problem is solved

  • Use this to quickly create relatable and aspirational content

  • Focus on emotions and real-life results, not features

  • Great for carousel posts, testimonial videos, and email intros

Example:

    • Before: “I used to dread networking events and never knew how to talk about myself.”

    • After: “Now, I get invited to speak at conferences — and people remember my story.”

    • Bridge: “The difference? I finally learned how to tell my story with confidence.”

3. PAS (Problem – Agitate – Solution)

Best for: Copywriting, email subject lines, landing pages, ad scripts, lead magnets.

What It Is:

PAS is a classic copywriting formula that first gets the audience’s attention with a problem, turns up the heat on the emotional impact of that problem, and then offers a solution — which, of course, is your brand/product. It’s especially useful when you want to create urgency or tension that motivates action.

 The Structure:

  • Problem: Identify a pain point or challenge your audience feels

  • Agitate: Emphasize the frustration, risk, or cost of not fixing it

  • Solution: Offer relief or a better path forward (your product/service)

How to Use It in Digital Marketing:

  • Use it in ads, sales pages, and emails to get immediate attention

  • Keep the problem relatable and the agitation empathetic — not fear-mongering

  • Focus your solution on value and transformation

Example:

  • Ad for a time-tracking app targeting freelancers:

    • Problem: “Still guessing where your work hours go each week?”

    • Agitate: “Unbilled hours are costing you hundreds of dollars a month — and your sanity.”

    • Solution: “Track every second with [App Name] and finally get paid what you’re worth.”
