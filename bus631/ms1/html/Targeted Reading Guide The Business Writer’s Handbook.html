Targeted Reading Guide: The Business Writer’s Handbook

As you develop stakeholder personas and craft tailored messages in this module, you’ll need to make sharp, intentional choices about tone, word choice, message structure, and audience fit. The Business Writer’s Handbook is a great tool for helping you make those choices with clarity and professionalism.

Below are the most useful sections for your work in this module—briefly explained and connected to your assignments.

1. Audience

Look for: "Analyzing the audience," "Understanding audience needs," and "Writing for multiple audiences"

Why it matters:
Understanding your audience is the foundation of effective communication. This section gives you tools to consider who your reader is, what they already know, what they value, and what tone or content they will expect.

How to use it:
Refer to this when developing your stakeholder personas or revising your messages in the milestone assignment. Pay attention to the list of audience characteristics (job role, knowledge level, attitude, reading habits).

2. Tone

Look for: "Tone and attitude in writing," "Maintaining professionalism," and "Adjusting tone to fit the audience"

Why it matters:
Tone shapes how your message is received. This section helps you calibrate your tone—whether it needs to be formal, conversational, assertive, or empathetic—based on your audience and purpose.

How to use it:
Use this section while writing messages to different audiences in your activities and milestone. Ask: Does this tone align with what my audience expects and needs?

3. Clarity

Look for: "Writing clearly and concisely," "Avoiding jargon," and "Using plain language"

Why it matters:
Audiences are busy and easily overwhelmed. Clear writing increases trust and reduces confusion. This section offers concrete tips on how to eliminate clutter, vague phrases, and confusing sentence structures.

How to use it:
Use this during message drafting and revision—especially when translating complex ideas for non-expert audiences. Clarity is key when tailoring messages for different stakeholder groups.

4. Word Choice and Diction

Look for: "Choosing the right words," "Concrete vs. abstract language," and "Clichés and buzzwords to avoid"

Why it matters:
The words you choose reveal how well you know your audience. This section helps you avoid overused phrases, unclear language, and misleading terminology.

How to use it:
When writing for different generational, cultural, or professional groups, check this section to ensure you're using accessible, relevant language that resonates.

5. Revising and Editing

Look for: "Steps for revising," "Peer review strategies," and "Editing for tone and audience alignment"

Why it matters:
Your first draft is rarely your best draft. This section offers practical strategies for refining your message, with a specific focus on audience alignment and clarity.

How to use it:
Apply this section when polishing your stakeholder messages or preparing your milestone submission. It’s also a great tool for the optional peer review assignment.
