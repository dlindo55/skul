The Business Writer’s Handbook

Essential Entries for Strategic Messaging, Crisis Response, and Public Trust

As you develop your Mini Strategic Communication Plan, especially if you’re addressing a crisis, leadership change, or sensitive organizational shift, these entries will guide how to write clearly, ethically, and credibly under pressure.

1. Credibility

What It Covers:

  • Establishing and maintaining trust in your message and your voice as a communicator.

  • Using evidence-based writing to support claims (data, real examples, testimonials).

  • Avoiding overstatements, vague generalizations, and emotionally manipulative language.

  • Understanding the link between clarity, tone, and perceived honesty.

Why It Matters:
Credibility is the foundation of all strategic communication. If your audience doesn’t trust you, the message doesn’t matter—even if it’s true. This is especially critical during high-stakes or emotionally charged communication moments.

Pro Tip from the Handbook:
Use facts, cite reliable sources when needed, and avoid hyperbole. Present potential objections with empathy to build trust.

2. Crisis Response

What It Covers:

  • Structuring a clear, calm, and honest message during times of uncertainty or disruption.

  • Knowing when to speak, what to say, and how to balance transparency with discretion.

  • Acknowledging responsibility without assigning blame too quickly or defensively.

  • Reassuring audiences while outlining next steps and organizational accountability.

Why It Matters:
In crisis communication, how you say something is just as important as what you say. Your audience is often emotional, confused, or looking for leadership—so messages must be clear, timely, and stabilizing.

Pro Tip from the Handbook:
Use active voice, avoid jargon, and always provide a way forward. Anticipate questions and offer assurance where possible.

3. Public Statements

What It Covers:

  • Writing official, external-facing messages such as press releases, executive statements, or company announcements.

  • Choosing the right format, structure, and tone based on the audience and context.

  • Opening with purpose, stating key facts, addressing the impact, and closing with the next step or call to action.

  • Ensuring messages are brand-aligned and professional even under pressure.

Why It Matters:
Public statements shape public perception. They must reflect the company’s values, voice, and professionalism—whether you're launching something new or responding to scrutiny.

Pro Tip from the Handbook:
Begin with the most important information first, then expand with context. Always assume your message will be quoted, forwarded, or posted publicly—so choose every word with care.
