The Business Writer’s Handbook

Key Entries for Digital Business Communication

Focus: Email • Virtual Meetings • Collaborative Communication

As digital platforms dominate modern business communication, it’s critical to use these tools clearly, professionally, and strategically. The entries below help you understand the written and spoken expectations in virtual work environments—and how to lead with clarity and credibility in every format.

Email

What It Covers:

  • Structuring effective business emails with clear subject lines, brief openings, focused body content, and actionable closes

  • Best practices for tone, formatting, and etiquette

  • Common pitfalls (e.g., vague requests, reply-all misuse, emotional replies)

Why It Matters:
Email remains the most common professional communication tool, but it’s often misused or misunderstood. A well-written email can save time, prevent confusion, and demonstrate leadership—even in routine exchanges.

Tips from the Handbook:

  • Use descriptive subject lines (“Q2 Budget Review – Action Needed by Friday”)

  • Keep paragraphs short and skimmable

  • Avoid sarcasm, excessive exclamation points, or emotional language

When to Use Email:
For documentation, asynchronous updates, formal requests, or messages that don’t require immediate response.

Virtual Meetings

What It Covers:

  • Preparing, facilitating, and participating in video or conference calls professionally

  • Setting and sticking to agendas

  • Managing time, engaging participants, and following up

Why It Matters:
Virtual meetings are often criticized for being inefficient or draining. This section helps you lead or contribute to meetings with purpose, keeping communication clear, inclusive, and action-oriented.

Tips from the Handbook:

  • Share the agenda in advance

  • Begin with a clear objective and expected outcomes

  • Summarize decisions and next steps before ending the meeting

  • Follow up with a concise written recap when appropriate

Collaborative Communication

What It Covers:

  • Writing and contributing in shared environments (e.g., shared docs, project boards, Slack, Teams)

  • Tone and clarity in real-time collaboration

  • Respectful commenting, version control, and shared accountability

Why It Matters:
Most business writing now happens in shared spaces, not solo documents. Collaborative communication requires transparency, emotional intelligence, and a strong grasp of audience dynamics—especially across functions or hierarchies.

Tips from the Handbook:

  • Be clear and direct without being abrupt

  • Use commenting tools constructively—suggest, don’t criticize

  • Confirm changes and responsibilities in writing

  • Keep communication professional, even in informal channels
