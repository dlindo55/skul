The Business Writer’s Handbook

Summary: Intercultural Communication • Brand Voice • Global Tone

Intercultural Communication

What It Covers:

  • The influence of cultural values, norms, and expectations on message delivery and interpretation

  • Adjusting tone, structure, and content for international or multicultural audiences

  • The importance of avoiding assumptions, idioms, humor, and cultural references that may not translate

Why It Matters:
Intercultural miscommunication can cause confusion, offense, or misalignment with global audiences. Strong communicators learn to adapt while staying clear, respectful, and inclusive.

Tips from the Handbook:

  • Be mindful of formality, hierarchy, and feedback styles

  • Provide context where needed, especially for audiences who may not share your background

  • Clarify terms and avoid jargon unless it is globally recognized

 Brand Voice

What It Covers:

  • Creating and maintaining a consistent tone and personality across all communication channels

  • Aligning message tone with the organization’s mission, audience, and values

  • Adjusting voice slightly across regions while maintaining core brand identity

Why It Matters:
A clear brand voice builds trust, recognition, and credibility. But in global communication, it must also be flexible enough to respect cultural expectations without losing its identity.

Tips from the Handbook:

  • Identify key brand voice attributes (e.g., bold, warm, professional)

  • Create internal voice guidelines for messaging consistency across languages and regions

  • Use localized examples while keeping tone aligned with brand values

Global Tone

What It Covers:

  • Choosing tone carefully in international communication, especially when addressing sensitive issues or diverse audiences

  • Understanding how tone expectations vary across cultures (e.g., formal vs. informal, direct vs. nuanced)

  • Using neutral, inclusive, and culturally adaptable language in multinational settings

Why It Matters:
A message that feels friendly and approachable in one culture may seem inappropriate or unprofessional in another. Getting the tone right helps avoid misunderstandings and strengthens relationships.

Tips from the Handbook:

  • Use plain, respectful language with a global audience

  • When unsure, default to professionalism and clarity over casualness

  • Test tone with a culturally diverse colleague or tool before sending critical communications
