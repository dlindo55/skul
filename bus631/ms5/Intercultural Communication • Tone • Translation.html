Intercultural Communication

What It Covers:

  • Awareness of how cultural values, customs, and assumptions affect how people interpret messages

  • Strategies for navigating differences in hierarchy, formality, nonverbal cues, and message structure

  • Avoiding ethnocentrism and approaching global communication with respect and adaptability

Why It Matters:
Miscommunication can happen easily across cultures—even when everyone is speaking the same language. Understanding cultural context helps prevent confusion, offense, or mistrust in international settings.

Tips from the Handbook:

  • Learn basic etiquette and business customs before communicating across cultures

  • Consider how formality, emotional expression, or hierarchy influence tone and format

  • When in doubt, default to clarity and professionalism

Tone

What It Covers:

  • Tone is the attitude or emotional undercurrent of your writing (e.g., confident, respectful, collaborative, assertive)

  • Tone should reflect both your purpose and your audience’s expectations

  • Tone shifts across cultures—what feels confident in one culture may feel arrogant in another

Why It Matters in Global Communication:
Tone is often where misunderstandings arise. A casual tone may seem unprofessional in high-formality cultures, while a direct tone may be perceived as aggressive in indirect-communication cultures.

Tips from the Handbook:

  • Keep tone neutral and professional unless you know the audience well

  • Use courteous language, especially when writing across levels or borders

  • Avoid humor, idioms, or sarcasm unless you're sure they'll be understood

Translation & Clarity for Global Audiences

What It Covers:

  • Writing for readers whose first language may not be English

  • Avoiding culturally specific metaphors, idioms, or jargon

  • Emphasizing clear structure, logical flow, and universal vocabulary

Why It Matters:
Even within multinational companies where English is the shared language, your writing must be inclusive, readable, and culturally adaptable.

Tips from the Handbook:

  • Use short, clear sentences and standard English grammar

  • Replace idioms (“hit the ground running”) with literal alternatives (“start quickly”)

  • Use visuals or formatting (bullets, headings, summaries) to support comprehension

  • Always proofread for ambiguity, especially when clarity is critical
